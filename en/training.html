Professional development · schools in the canton of Vaud and French-speaking Switzerland

AI training for your teaching team, from a teacher in the canton of Vaud

Three formats for school leadership teams and teaching staff. No tool to sell.

The situation

Generative AI is already in your classrooms. The Swiss recommendations exist, but between the official texts and daily practice your teachers are left alone with concrete questions. What can I ask of my students? What still measures learning? What am I allowed to do with their data?

I teach in Vaud classrooms and I do education research on AI in schools. I have no tool to sell. I help your team decide what it does about AI, within the real institutional framework and with tasks that come from real classrooms.

Three formats

Format A · entry point

AI in the classroom. What the official texts say, what actually works.

Talk and workshop · 90 minutes · staff meeting or the opening of a professional development day

Establish a shared factual basis on what generative AI does and does not do, set out the Swiss institutional framework, and lower the tension between enthusiasts and sceptics.

Structure

  • 20 min — capabilities and limits, demonstrated live on real curriculum tasks
  • 25 min — the institutional framework: what the official texts permit, require and forbid
  • 25 min — three classroom-ready uses and three things not to do
  • 20 min — questions, structured around the most common objections

Your teachers leave with

  • a one-page summary of the official Swiss texts
  • a do-and-don’t sheet on student data
  • three task templates aligned with the curriculum

Format B · hands-on workshop

Reworking your own assessments

Half day · 3 to 3.5 hours · groups by cycle

Each teacher brings their own material. The aim is that they leave with one of their lesson sequences or assessments reworked for a context where students have access to AI, and that they have handled a tool themselves within a compliant setting.

Structure

  • short input, a condensed version of format A
  • guided hands-on work on a real sequence the teacher brings
  • redesigning an assessment: what still measures learning when students have access to AI?
  • peer critique and individual commitments

Your teachers leave with

  • one reworked task per participant
  • an assessment redesign checklist
  • a bank of prompt patterns aligned with the curriculum
  • a follow-up email after four weeks

Format C · course

Through to an AI use policy for your school

Three 90-minute sessions across one term · classroom micro-experiments in between

The literature on professional development is consistent on this point: one-off sessions rarely change practice, while sustained formats anchored in practice do. This is the format that produces a lasting artefact for the school.

Structure

  • session 1 — shared basis and a first classroom experiment assigned
  • session 2 — debrief of what happened in the classrooms, going deeper on assessment and differentiation
  • session 3 — consolidation and drafting of an AI use policy

Your school leaves with

  • a draft AI use policy
  • a log of the experiments run in classrooms
  • a resource bank

Data protection

This is the question every school leader asks, and it is addressed first in every format.

The baseline rule: no personal student data — no scripts, names, marks or individual situations — goes into a consumer tool. Demonstrations and workshops use only anonymised or fictional examples, and every participant receives a written do-and-don’t sheet.

I propose no use that would fall outside cantonal directives, and I say so in the room when a common practice does not meet them.

Frequently asked questions

HEP Vaud already runs AI training. Why bring in someone else?
I work from the same official Swiss framework. What I add is a session built for your school, on the date of your professional development day, using material from your own cycles, delivered by someone currently teaching in Vaud classrooms. This complements the cantonal provision rather than replacing it.
What about our students’ data?
It is the first point covered in every format. The baseline rule is that no personal student data goes into a consumer tool. I work only with anonymised or fictional examples and I provide a written do-and-don’t sheet.
Our teachers are deeply divided about AI. Is that a problem?
No, and the research documents it. The format is designed for divided teams: nobody is converted, the session establishes facts, a framework and professional choices.
Which tool do you recommend?
None in particular. I have no commercial relationship with any vendor and no licence to sell. I cover categories of use and the criteria for choosing within cantonal rules.
What does a session cost?
On request, depending on the format, the number of participants and travel. The first sessions are delivered at a reduced pilot rate in exchange for written participant evaluations.
